# Multiplex Serological Profiling Reveals Diverse Avian and Mammalian Influenza A Virus Exposure in Swine

## Overview
This repository contains the code and data used for the study:

**"Multiplex Serological Profiling Reveals Diverse Avian and Mammalian Influenza A Virus Exposure in Swine"**

This study investigates influenza A virus (IAV) exposure in pigs in Cambodia using large-scale serological surveillance. Pigs are recognized as important hosts for influenza viruses originating from humans, birds, and other mammals, making them key intermediaries in the emergence of novel pandemic strains.

To better understand viral exposure in swine populations, we analyzed pig serum samples collected between March 2020 and July 2022 using a multiplex microsphere immunoassay targeting a broad panel of influenza hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) antigens. This approach enabled the detection of antibodies against multiple influenza virus lineages simultaneously, providing insights into the diversity of influenza viruses circulating at the human–animal–avian interface.


## Key Findings

- A total of 4,089 pig serum samples were collected in Cambodia, with 1,321 samples (32.5%) testing positive for influenza A virus antibodies by ELISA.
- A multiplex microsphere immunoassay was developed to detect antibodies against a wide range of HA and NA antigens.
- Seroprevalence was highest for pandemic H1N1/pdm09 (35.2–45.7%), indicating substantial exposure to human-origin influenza viruses.
- Lower seroprevalence was observed for classical swine H1 (16.4–17.7%) and European avian-like swine H1 (~15%) lineages.
- Antibody responses suggested lineage-specific and mixed exposures, highlighting complex infection histories in pig populations.
- H3 responses varied by clade, suggesting circulation of genetically diverse viruses.
- Antibodies against avian-origin H5, H7, and H9 viruses were detected in <8% of influenza-positive samples, indicating occasional exposure to avian influenza viruses.
---

## Data Sources
- Pig serum samples (n = 4,089) collected from swine populations in Cambodia between March 2020 and July 2022.
- Serological screening using influenza A ELISA followed by multiplex microsphere immunoassay targeting multiple hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) antigens.
- Analysis aimed at characterizing lineage-specific influenza exposure and cross-lineage immune responses in swine populations.

---

## Implications
The findings reveal extensive exposure of pigs to multiple influenza A virus lineages, including human-origin pandemic viruses and avian-associated strains. These results highlight the complex ecology of influenza viruses in swine and underscore the importance of integrated serological and genomic surveillance to monitor zoonotic risk and detect emerging pandemic threats.
